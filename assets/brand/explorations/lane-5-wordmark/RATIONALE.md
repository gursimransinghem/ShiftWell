# Lane 5 — Geometric Wordmark / SW Ligature
## Rationale

---

### The idea

A custom SW ligature where S and W share a single vertical upstroke — the ligature joint. The brand owns two letters, one joint, one purple pixel. That is the entire system.

---

### The ligature mechanic

The S is constructed from two counter-rotating arcs, each governed by a 20-unit construction circle. The W is built from five nodes across a 84-unit horizontal span, with a deliberate asymmetry: the center peak (node C) rises to 26 units, not to cap-height. This asymmetry is the branded detail — it prevents the W from reading as a mirror-flipped M, and it keeps the waveform profile from being the cliché "mountain range." The ligature joint is the S's lower-right terminal extended upward to the W's top-left entry point — a single vertical stroke at x=64, shared by both letters. That stroke is rendered in #7B61FF (accent purple) — the one moment of color in the entire mark. Its conceptual meaning: the boundary between states. The shift between awake and asleep. Between one schedule and the next. It is not explained anywhere. It does not need to be.

---

### Why people would wear it

The mark is abstract enough to be owned — no viewer would mistake it for a generic healthcare brand — but direct enough to be legible. It looks like it belongs on a precision instrument, not a wellness Instagram post. A shift worker wearing it signals that they use a serious tool, not a supplement.

---

### Scalability notes

The 32×32 favicon is where wordmarks die. This one survives because the purple joint acts as a visual divider: at 32px, the eye reads "curved mass left of the line, angular mass right of the line" and resolves it as S and W. The strokes at 32px are 2.5px wide — at the minimum for screen rendering. Below 24px the joint would need to be dropped; that is acceptable (24px favicons are rare, typically 16px ICO files, which should use a simpler monogram). At 32px this reads. Tested mentally at 1×, 2×, and 3× density.

---

### Anti-cliché checklist

- No initials in a circle or badge: the mark is a freestanding ligature, not contained
- No Comic Sans / Papyrus / fun fonts: no text fonts used in the mark itself
- No serif wordmark with gold foil: Inter 700, white, zero foil
- No over-italicized letterforms: mark is upright, geometric
- No swooshes through letters: the purple joint is vertical, structural, not decorative
- No sleep iconography (z's, pillows): zero — the waveform reading is incidental, not the concept
- Survives 1-color monochrome at 32×32: yes — remove purple joint and the S/W still reads
- SW readable on first glance: yes — both letterforms are full-weight, no decoding required

---

### What I deliberately didn't do

Did not put a moon, a bed, a wave, or a horizon in the letterforms. The brief mentioned these as possibilities — I rejected them because they would make the mark illustrative rather than typographic. The W's four strokes already have an organic waveform quality at the center peak; making that explicit would reduce the mark from a brand to a rebus. Did not use the gold accent in the mark — gold is reserved for the fulcrum divider system. The purple joint is the only accent because the joint is the concept: the moment of transition. One concept. One color. One pixel of meaning.
